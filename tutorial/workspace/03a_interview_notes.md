# Interview and observation notes (I1–I8, O1–O2)

> **Note to the instructor: simulated research.** I did not have access to real restaurant managers,
> so I used an AI agent to role-play eight managers answering my final interview guide
> (`02_interview_guide_iterate.md`), and two dinner-service observations. I gave each manager a
> different restaurant profile. Every fact below is simulated, not field data.

## Interviews

**I1: Linh, night manager, 40-seat noodle restaurant (owner absent in the evening)**
- Works the evening shift alone with 2 cooks and 1 server; covers host, cashier and expediting.
- Delivery orders arrive on a tablet at the counter; she reads them aloud to the kitchen.
- "When the tablet beeps and I'm at a table, the order just waits. Sometimes it auto-cancels."
- Marks dishes as sold out by phoning the platform's support line; it takes 10+ minutes.
- Success = "no cancelled orders and no angry calls from the owner the next morning".

**I2: Tuan, day manager, café chain branch**
- Has a dedicated staff member for delivery at lunch; delivery is about 40% of sales.
- Uses the tablet mostly to accept orders and print tickets; prefers paper tickets in the kitchen.
- Riders often come to the wrong counter; staff shout order numbers.
- Values speed of acceptance because the platform ranks fast restaurants higher.

**I3: Hoa, evening manager, family-run rice restaurant**
- Short-staffed after 6pm; she cooks some dishes herself during peak.
- Glances at the tablet "from two metres away while plating"; small text is hard to read.
- Shared Wi-Fi with customers drops at dinner time; orders sometimes appear late in a burst.
- Has had riders leave with the wrong bag twice this month.

**I4: Khang, manager, pizza shop with 2 locations**
- Busiest time is 6–9pm; at peak he "pauses" delivery by turning the tablet off.
- Would like to tell customers about a longer prep time rather than disappear from the app.
- Kitchen tracks orders on a whiteboard; the tablet shows nothing after acceptance.

**I5: Mai, night manager, bubble tea and snacks**
- Works alone with one helper after 8pm; mostly delivery orders.
- Customers message her about missing toppings; she refunds by asking the platform via chat.
- Wants to see which orders are late before the customer complains.
- Does not trust the tablet's ready time; she estimates herself.

**I6: Quang, floor manager, upscale Vietnamese restaurant**
- Delivery is under 15% of sales; dine-in guests come first.
- Riders waiting inside the dining room bother guests; he asks them to wait outside.
- Rarely touches the tablet; a host accepts orders.

**I7: Thu, shift manager, fried chicken franchise**
- Follows franchise procedures; every order must be checked against a bag checklist.
- Rider no-shows: food waits 20+ minutes and goes cold; she must call support to reassign.
- Complaints come from the franchise office the next day, not in real time.

**I8: Dung, night manager, hotpot and grill restaurant**
- Two cooks, one server and himself at night; owner reviews cancellation rate weekly.
- Accepts orders only when he is back at the counter; missed orders count against the restaurant.
- Item availability changes during the night (fresh seafood runs out); updating it is slow.
- "I need the tablet to work for me while I'm away from it."

## Observations (dinner service)

**O1: Noodle restaurant (I1's site), Tuesday 6–9pm**
- Tablet sat on the counter next to the cash register; Linh was away from it most of the time.
- 3 orders waited over 2 minutes before acceptance; 1 timed out and was cancelled.
- Wi-Fi dropped twice for about a minute; two orders appeared together afterwards.
- A rider waited 8 minutes; Linh looked at three paper tickets to find his order.

**O2: Hotpot restaurant (I8's site), Friday 6–9pm**
- Dung marked a dish sold out after two customers had already ordered it; he called both customers.
- The kitchen asked "is order 4 picked up?" several times; there was no shared status.
- A rider took an order without any check of the order number.
- Dung handled a refund request by phone with the platform; it took about 12 minutes.
